## The Control Habits That Speed Up WashTheSkyscraper Badges

This experience only asks for three equipment actions: use the tool, rotate the tool, and open upgrades. Everything else is movement and camera. When those three inputs become automatic, badge progress turns into a question of time rather than skill. When they are not, you spend entire sessions fighting your own hands instead of cleaning glass.

| Action | PC | Controller | Why badge hunters care |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Use equipment | Left click | RT (right trigger) | The only input that actually cleans a pane |
| Rotate equipment | R | X | Matches the blade to tall glass, corners, and thin edges |
| Open upgrades | U | D-pad Down | Buys cleaning speed and reach when a run bottlenecks |
| Move | WASD | Left stick | Lines up strokes and repositions along a ledge |
| Camera | Mouse | Right stick | Aims, and angles along glass to spot missed streaks |

Hold, do not mash. A quick tap on the use input reads as a short burst — fine for a small spot, useless for a full pane. Ease onto the surface, hold through the pass, release at the end. If your spray keeps dying instantly, you are tapping. If your squeegee smears, you are tapping while pulling.

Rotate before the stroke, not during it. Default orientation is usually right for a wall directly in front of you and wrong for most windows. Rotate, make one slow pass, and keep that orientation until the surface shape changes. When you swap from washer to squeegee, reset the rotation so you do not carry a vertical blade onto a horizontal ledge.

Buy upgrades with a sentence, not a feeling. "Too slow" and "cannot reach" are reasons; "it looks good" is how players end up standing in a menu on a ledge while their co-op crew cleans the next band without them. Close the shop, test the purchase on one pane, then decide whether to buy again.

## A Badge-Friendly Session Plan

Do not grind the whole tower just to learn your inputs. Run short drills first, then play a real run, then check the badge menu. Ten minutes of focused practice is worth an hour of confused climbing.

| Drill | Setup | Time | What it trains |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Washer field | One dirty wall, use input only, shop closed | 5–10 min | Spray control and overlapping passes |
| Rotation reps | Alternate vertical and horizontal panes, rotate between every pane | 5 min | Muscle memory for the rotate bind |
| Edge pass | Start at one gasket, pull through to the opposite gasket, overlap the next stroke | 5–10 min | Finish-quality strokes that survive a cleaning check |
| Band walk | One band of floors end to end, no shopping | 10 min | Pacing and route order |

Then run the real thing: pick a band, finish it completely, and only afterward open the badge menu to see what moved. Working band by band instead of sniping random windows also makes it obvious which surfaces you skipped — which matters a great deal if a completion-style badge checks the whole facade rather than a single pane.

- Overlap every stroke slightly so no dirty strip survives between passes.
- Work gasket to gasket rather than spiraling around the center of a window.
- Look along the glass from an angle after each pass; streaks invisible head-on show up as lines.
- Reset rotation whenever the surface changes shape.

If you play with others, split roles. One cleaner takes washer duty on the big glass while another handles squeegee work and edges. Servers hold up to 20 players, so inputs you can use without looking matter far more in a crowd than they do in a solo sandbox.

## Mistakes That Quietly Cost You Badge Progress

Most badge frustration is aim frustration wearing a control costume. The binds work fine; the stroke does not. Stand close enough that the washer cone or squeegee blade clearly cuts into the surface — from too far away the input still fires, but the whole pass is spent on air.

| Mistake | What it looks like | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mashing the use input | Spray dies instantly, squeegee smears | Hold through a full pass |
| Diagonal blade on rectangular glass | Streaks at corners and along gaskets | Rotate so the blade's long edge matches the pane's long edge |
| Living in the upgrade menu | You are on a ledge, not on glass | Buy with a reason, close, test on one pane |
| Random window order | Dirty frames and skipped edges | Work band by band, gasket to gasket |
| Trusting a stale badge list | "Why did nothing unlock?" | Compare against the in-game menu and check patch notes |

On controller, make the stroke with the left stick and leave the camera alone during a pull; right-stick drift is what turns a clean pass into a curved smear. On PC, keep the mouse still during a held squeegee stroke unless you are deliberately tracing a curve.

## FAQ: WashTheSkyscraper Badges

**How many WashTheSkyscraper badges are there?**
There is no reliable fixed number worth quoting. Badge sets get added and retuned across updates, so the count inside the in-game badge menu is the only current answer. If you are planning a long grind, screenshot that menu first so you can compare before and after.

**Can I unlock badges solo?**
Many triggers are simple cleaning milestones, which are perfectly solo-friendly. Social badges that require other players in the same server are a different story. Read the criteria in the menu before assuming a solo session will flip them.

**Do upgrades help me earn badges faster?**
Indirectly. Cleaning speed and reach shorten the road to milestone and completion triggers, but badges track outcomes rather than purchases. Upgrade when you can name the bottleneck — too slow, or cannot reach — and test the result on a single pane before buying again.

**Do promo codes unlock badges?**
Generally no. Codes, when they exist at all, tend to hand out coins or temporary boosts rather than badge rewards. Anything you see on a code list counts as community reporting until you confirm it in your own game, so verify before building a session around it.

If you want the technical background on how experiences award badges in the first place, [Roblox's official developer documentation on badge systems](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/badges) explains the awarding rules that every experience builds on.
